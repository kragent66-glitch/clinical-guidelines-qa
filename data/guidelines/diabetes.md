# ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes (2026)

## SECTION 1: Screening and Diagnosis
Recommended screening criteria and diagnostic thresholds.

*   **Screening**: Screen all adults starting at age 35, or earlier if overweight (BMI >= 25 kg/m2) and have additional risk factors. Repeat every 3 years if normal.
*   **Diagnostic Thresholds**:
    *   **Pre-Diabetes**: HbA1c 5.7% - 6.4%, or Fasting Plasma Glucose (FPG) 100 - 125 mg/dL.
    *   **Type 2 Diabetes**: HbA1c >= 6.5%, or FPG >= 126 mg/dL (confirmed on separate day).

---

## SECTION 2: Pharmacological Treatment Pathway
First-line and escalation pathways for glycemic control.

*   **First-line Therapy**: Metformin 500mg daily, titrate to 1000mg twice daily as tolerated. If contraindicated or not tolerated, consider SGLT2 inhibitors or GLP-1 receptor agonists.
*   **Cardiovascular/Renal Risk**: If ASCVD, Heart Failure, or CKD is present, first-line therapy should include an SGLT2 inhibitor (e.g., Empagliflozin 10-25mg daily) or a GLP-1 RA (e.g., Semaglutide 0.25-1mg weekly) regardless of baseline HbA1c.
*   **Dual Therapy Escalation**: If HbA1c remains above target (>7.0%) after 3 months, add a second agent.
    *   **Options**: GLP-1 RA, SGLT2i, DPP-4i (e.g., Sitagliptin 100mg daily), or Basal Insulin (Glargine 10 units daily starting dose).

---

## SECTION 3: Special Clinical Warnings
*   **Hypoglycemia Risk**: Educate patients on symptoms (sweating, shaking, confusion) and rule of 15 (15g fast-acting carbs, recheck in 15 mins).
*   **Lactic Acidosis**: Metformin carries a boxed warning for lactic acidosis. Avoid if eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m2.
*   **Euglycemic DKA**: SGLT2 inhibitors can cause diabetic ketoacidosis with normal glucose levels. Hold SGLT2i 3 days prior to major surgery or during acute illness.
