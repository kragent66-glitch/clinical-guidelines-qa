# ACC/AHA Hypertension Guidelines (2026)

## SECTION 1: Classification and Screening
Standard blood pressure categories and screening criteria.

*   **Classification**:
    *   **Normal**: < 120/80 mmHg.
    *   **Elevated**: 120-129 / < 80 mmHg.
    *   **Stage 1 Hypertension**: 130-139 or 80-89 mmHg.
    *   **Stage 2 Hypertension**: >= 140 or >= 90 mmHg.
*   **Measurement**: Confirm with out-of-office measurements (ABPM or HBPM) before starting pharmacological therapy.

---

## SECTION 2: Treatment Pathway
First-line agents and escalation thresholds.

*   **Elevated BP**: Non-pharmacological (lifestyle modification) only. Reassess in 3-6 months.
*   **Stage 1 Hypertension**:
    *   **Low Risk (10-yr ASCVD < 10%)**: Lifestyle modifications, reassess in 3-6 months.
    *   **High Risk (10-yr ASCVD >= 10%, or CKD, Diabetes)**: Start 1 first-line drug. Target < 130/80 mmHg.
*   **Stage 2 Hypertension**: Start 2 first-line drugs of different classes. Target < 130/80 mmHg.
*   **First-line Drug Classes**:
    *   **Thiazide Diuretics**: Chlorthalidone 12.5-25mg daily, or Hydrochlorothiazide 12.5-50mg daily.
    *   **ACE Inhibitors**: Lisinopril 10-40mg daily.
    *   **ARBs**: Losartan 50-100mg daily.
    *   **CCBs**: Amlodipine 2.5-10mg daily.

---

## SECTION 3: Clinical Warnings and Contraindications
*   **Pregnancy Warning**: ACE inhibitors, ARBs, and Direct Renin Inhibitors (Aliskiren) are strictly contraindicated due to fetal toxicity. Use Labetalol or Nifedipine instead.
*   **Hyperkalemia**: ACEIs and ARBs can increase potassium. Monitor potassium and serum creatinine within 1-2 weeks of initiation.
*   **Gout**: Thiazide diuretics can elevate uric acid and precipitate gout attacks. Avoid or use with caution.
