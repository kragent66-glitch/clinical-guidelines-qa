# ESMO Clinical Practice Guidelines - Lung Cancer (2026)

## SECTION 1: Diagnosis and Staging
Confirming diagnosis and determining the extent of disease.

*   **Diagnostic Confirmation**: Tissue biopsy confirming malignancy (e.g., adenocarcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, small cell lung cancer) via histology or cytology. Molecular testing is essential for NSCLC.
*   **Staging (TNM System)**: Based on Tumor size/invasion (T), Lymph Node involvement (N), and Metastasis (M). Crucial for treatment decisions.
    *   **Limited Stage SCLC**: Confined to one hemithorax and regional lymph nodes. Treatable with chemotherapy +/- radiation.
    *   **Extensive Stage SCLC**: Disease outside hemithorax or supraclavicular nodes. Treat with chemotherapy +/- immunotherapy.

---

## SECTION 2: Treatment Pathways

*   **Early-Stage NSCLC (Stage I-II)**: Surgery (lobectomy/pneumonectomy) is primary treatment. Adjuvant chemotherapy may be considered for Stage II.
*   **Locally Advanced NSCLC (Stage III)**: Treatment depends on resectability. Options include chemoradiation, surgery + adjuvant chemotherapy, or definitive chemoradiation.
*   **Metastatic NSCLC (Stage IV)**: Systemic therapy is the mainstay.
    *   **EGFR/ALK/ROS1/BRAF mutations**: Targetable driver mutations warrant first-line tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs). E.g., Osimertinib for EGFR mutations.
    *   **No Targetable Mutations**: First-line options include chemotherapy (e.g., Cisplatin/Pemetrexed) +/- immunotherapy (e.g., Pembrolizumab).
*   **Small Cell Lung Cancer (SCLC)**:
    *   **Limited Stage**: Chemotherapy (Etoposide + Platinum) + Thoracic Radiation.
    *   **Extensive Stage**: Chemotherapy (Etoposide + Platinum) + Immunotherapy (e.g., Atezolizumab or Durvalumab).

---

## SECTION 3: Clinical Warnings and Considerations
*   **Molecular Testing Warning**: Mandatory for NSCLC. Results guide targeted therapy selection and significantly impact prognosis and treatment choice. Missing mutations leads to suboptimal treatment.
*   **Immunotherapy Warning**: Check for autoimmune conditions before starting immunotherapy. Monitor for immune-related adverse events (irAEs) like pneumonitis, colitis, hepatitis, endocrinopathies. Hold/discontinue based on irAE severity.
*   **Oncologic Emergencies**: Be vigilant for potential complications like SVC syndrome, spinal cord compression, or malignant hypercalcemia. These require prompt recognition and management.
