# GINA Global Strategy for Asthma Management (2026)

## SECTION 1: Diagnosis and Severity Assessment
Criteria for confirming asthma and assessing control.

*   **Diagnosis**: Variable expiratory airflow limitation confirmed by spirometry (FEV1/FVC ratio reduced, FEV1 increases >12% and >200mL after bronchodilator).
*   **Severity**: Assessed retrospectively based on the level of treatment required to control symptoms (Mild: Steps 1-2, Moderate: Steps 3-4, Severe: Step 5).

---

## SECTION 2: Pharmacological Treatment Steps
GINA Track 1 is the preferred approach (using low-dose ICS-formoterol as both reliever and controller).

*   **Step 1-2 (Mild Asthma)**: Preferred reliever is low-dose ICS-formoterol (e.g., Budesonide-Formoterol 160/4.5 mcg, 1 inhalation as-needed for symptom relief).
*   **Step 3 (Mild-to-Moderate)**: Low-dose maintenance and reliever ICS-formoterol (Budesonide-Formoterol 160/4.5 mcg, 1 inhalation daily plus as-needed reliever).
*   **Step 4 (Moderate-to-Severe)**: Medium-dose maintenance ICS-formoterol (Budesonide-Formoterol 160/4.5 mcg, 2 inhalations twice daily) or ICS-LABA combination.
*   **Step 5 (Severe)**: High-dose ICS-LABA + LAMA (e.g., Tiotropium) or biologic therapies (e.g., Omalizumab, Mepolizumab). Refer to specialist.

---

## SECTION 3: Clinical Warnings and Safety
*   **SABA Monotherapy Risk**: Over-reliance on short-acting beta-agonists (SABA, e.g., Albuterol) without inhaled corticosteroids (ICS) is associated with increased risk of asthma-related death. GINA no longer recommends SABA-only treatment.
*   **Oral Corticosteroids (OCS) Warning**: Limit systemic OCS use to short rescue bursts (e.g., Prednisone 40-50mg daily for 5-7 days) for severe exacerbations. Chronic use causes severe systemic side effects (osteoporosis, cataracts, diabetes).
*   **Inhalation Technique**: Incorrect inhaler technique is the most common cause of "uncontrolled" asthma. Always observe the patient's technique before escalating therapy.
